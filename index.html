What is a Carbon Footprint? (in simple words)

A carbon footprint is the total amount of greenhouse gases (mainly carbon dioxide, CO₂) that we release into the air because of what we do: using electricity, travelling, cooking, eating food and buying things. It is measured in kilograms (kg) or tonnes of CO₂ per year.

Think of it like a footprint on sand: every activity leaves a mark on the planet. The smaller the mark, the better for Earth.

⚡

Electricity

Most electricity comes from burning coal, which releases CO₂. Switching off unused fans and lights helps.

🚗

Transport

Petrol and diesel vehicles and flights produce a large part of our emissions.

🍽️

Food & Cooking

LPG for cooking and the food we eat also add to our footprint.

♻️

Waste

Reducing, reusing and recycling means less new material and less pollution.

💡 Did you know?

  • The average person in India emits about 2 tonnes of CO₂ per year.
  • The world average is about 4.7 tonnes per person per year.
  • One mature tree absorbs roughly 20 kg of CO₂ every year.
